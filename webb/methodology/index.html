How We Work

The Standard We Hold Ourselves To

Wiba Signals Editorial Standards Updated May 2026

Wiba Signals produces analysis for people who make decisions based on it. That means every claim we publish must be verifiable, every number must have a named origin, and every error must be corrected on the record.

This page describes the exact standards we apply. It exists so that readers, journalists, and researchers can assess whether our work meets the bar they require — and hold us accountable when it doesn't.

Source Requirements

Every numerical claim published on Wiba Signals must satisfy three conditions before it appears in print:

1. A named primary source. We do not publish figures attributed to "industry reports" or "research suggests." The source must be named — Eurostat, SCB, Stanford HAI, OECD, McKinsey Global Institute, WEF, Vinnova, Digg, ILO, or equivalent. If we cannot name it, we do not use it.

2. A year of publication. Undated figures do not appear in our analysis. AI adoption data from 2022 and 2025 describe different realities. We specify the year of every statistic and flag when comparing across surveys that use different definitions.

3. A confidence classification. We distinguish between three tiers:

High confidence

Direct figures from official statistics bodies (SCB, Eurostat, OECD). Cross-verified against at least one additional source. Used without qualification in our analysis.

Medium confidence

Survey data from reputable research organizations (McKinsey, WEF, Stanford HAI). Single-source. Used with the survey methodology noted.

Composite estimate

Figures derived by combining multiple surveys with different methodologies. Always labeled explicitly. Not used as the central claim in an argument — only as context.

Verification Process

Before any piece is published, every numerical claim passes through a structured verification sequence. This is not optional and not abbreviated when deadlines are tight.

Step 1 — Claim extraction. Every specific number, percentage, ranking, index position, and time-bound assertion in the draft is listed explicitly. If it sounds like a fact, it is treated as a claim that requires verification.

Step 2 — Source matching. Each claim is matched against our internal source archive or traced back to the named primary source directly. We verify the exact figure — not an approximation, not a rounded version.

Step 3 — Conflict resolution. If our source says something different from what is in the draft, the draft is corrected. If no source can be found, the claim is removed. We do not publish figures we cannot verify.

Claims that pass all three steps are cleared for publication. Claims that fail are either corrected with a verified figure or cut entirely.

What We Do Not Do

Equally important is what our standards prohibit:

No vendor alignment. We do not accept sponsored content, sponsored research, or relationships that create financial incentive to favor one technology vendor over another. Our analysis of Microsoft, Google, Anthropic, and others is conducted under identical standards regardless of market position.

No single-source central arguments. If our core claim depends entirely on one survey, we say so explicitly. We do not build arguments on single-source data without flagging the limitation.

No projections presented as facts. Forecasts are labeled as forecasts. We state the organization that produced the projection, the year it was published, and the assumptions it rests on where these are disclosed.

No silent corrections. If we publish an error and correct it, the correction is noted in the piece with the original figure, the correct figure, and the date of correction. We do not quietly edit published text.

Corrections and Challenges

If you find a figure that does not match its cited source, or a claim that cannot be verified, we want to know. Send the specific claim, the source you consulted, and what it actually says to christianbauhn@yahoo.se with the subject line "Data challenge."

We will investigate within five business days. If the challenge is valid, we correct the piece publicly. If it is not, we explain why in our reply.

This is not a courtesy — it is how the work improves.

These standards apply to all analysis published on wibasignals.com. They were established at the founding of Wiba Signals and are reviewed when new data categories or source types are introduced. Last reviewed: May 2026.
