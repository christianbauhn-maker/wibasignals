80% of Silicon Valley Open-Source AI Runs on Chinese Models

Wiba Signals · 20 May 2026 · The Market
  • Alibaba's Qwen model family has surpassed 700 million downloads. DeepSeek, Kimi, GLM, and MiniMax are widely deployed across production systems at US companies — often matching or outperforming Western models at a fraction of the cost.
  • Airbnb runs customer service on a Chinese open-source model. Pinterest built its recommendation engine on one. Andreessen Horowitz, the most influential VC firm in Silicon Valley, has backed multiple Chinese AI companies.
  • According to Statrys' on-the-ground reporting from Asia, approximately 80% of Silicon Valley startups working with open-source AI are currently building on Chinese models. The primary driver is return on investment, not political alignment.

The Adoption Pattern

The dominant narrative in Western media frames AI competition as US versus China — a geopolitical race where choosing a Chinese model is a security concern or an ideological statement. The engineers and investors actually making model selection decisions describe a different calculation.

Chinese open-source models — primarily Qwen (Alibaba), DeepSeek, Kimi, GLM (Tsinghua/Zhipu AI), and MiniMax — are free, open-weight, and perform at levels that match or exceed models from Anthropic, OpenAI, and Google on most standard benchmarks. The cost differential is substantial: using a Chinese open-source model costs a fraction of API calls to equivalent proprietary Western models.

For a startup optimising compute costs, that differential is decisive. The engineers who switched describe it as a straightforward business decision, made quietly, without announcements.

The Security and Censorship Question

The objections raised against Chinese AI models fall into two categories: data security and political censorship.

On censorship: the concern has a factual basis. Ask DeepSeek about Tiananmen Square and it redirects the query. The model reflects its training constraints on politically sensitive topics within China's regulatory environment.

On data security: the comparative picture is more complicated. ChatGPT (OpenAI) trains on user conversations by default. Google's Gemini integrates with Gmail, YouTube, and full Google account history. Anthropic updated its data retention policy to five years for some data categories. The claim that Western AI is categorically more privacy-protective than Chinese AI does not hold under comparison of actual data practices.

For organisations building on open-weight models — where the model weights are downloaded and run on their own infrastructure — the data privacy question is structurally different again: the model operator controls what data leaves the environment.

What This Means for AI Strategy

The bifurcation of global AI infrastructure into US-backed and China-backed stacks is happening at the foundation layer — model weights and training compute — while the application layer remains interoperable. A company running Qwen on its own infrastructure for customer service functions is not "using Chinese AI" in the geopolitical sense its critics imply; it is using a cheaper, capable model.

The strategic question for organisations is not "Chinese or Western?" It is: what is the actual risk surface of each deployment, and does that risk surface map onto the cost and capability differential?

Chinese capital flowing into AI at scale — described by Statrys as resembling the crypto investment wave of 2020–2021 but with companies generating actual revenue — suggests that the model capability gap between Chinese and Western AI is more likely to close than widen over the next 24 months.
